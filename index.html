Where I stand: product in the age of AI

When building gets cheap, deciding gets expensive.

AI is making software faster and cheaper to build. That moves the constraint upstream, to the decision about what to build, and makes that decision more costly to get wrong. Cheap building erodes the discipline of deliberately not building something, while every feature still has to be maintained, supported, explained and sold.

The advantage is speed with accuracy, not speed alone. So AI belongs first in discovery and decision-making, and then in the product itself: built in, not bolted on.

Product-thinking is core to rethinking how software is built.

In the product

MagicAI Chat is an agent that does more than answer: it books trial sessions, pauses contracts and changes memberships directly in the platform, around the clock, and hands over to staff with full context when a person is needed. MagicAI Engage predicts which members are at risk and reaches them on the channel they respond to. At Cleverbridge, the same idea came earlier: a model predicting subscription churn, with workflows that acted on it.

In how product works

At Sport Alliance I led the rebuild of how the product organisation works with AI. The team’s product harness, with more than 20 standardised skills connected to the codebase and to planning and customer tools, reached 100% adoption within three weeks, with 70% of the team becoming power users. Together with Engineering, we redesigned the development lifecycle so that AI-assisted discovery and specification feed straight into agentic delivery.

Where I make the difference

Some companies need a permanent product leader for this work, others an interim one with a defined mission, a fixed term and real authority. These are the situations I am brought into, in either role.

  • Post-acquisition product integration

    Two or more product lines after a deal, and a decision to make about what becomes one platform.

  • Building or rebuilding a product organisation

    Product has to become a function the business can rely on, with the leadership, cadence and governance that requires.

  • Portfolio simplification

    Too many products, too many commitments, and investment spread too thinly to move any of them.

  • Pre-exit product readiness

    Making the product, its roadmap and its economics legible to an acquirer or investor.

  • AI-enabling a product organisation

    Moving from scattered experiments to a standard way of working with AI across discovery, decisions and delivery.

I also advise boards, investors and leadership teams on these questions, drawing on a background that started in consulting.
